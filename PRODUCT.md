# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

The site serves public technical readers first, with recruiters and hiring managers as an important secondary audience. Readers arrive to understand Lyla's engineering thinking, practical projects, and professional credibility without needing a dense portfolio walkthrough.

## Product Purpose

This is a personal software engineering blog and portfolio. It should help readers quickly understand what Lyla builds, what she writes about, and why her work is worth following or hiring for. Success means the homepage makes a clear first impression, routes readers to strong posts and projects, and communicates technical competence with a human, independent voice.

## Brand Personality

Modern, straightforward, creative, and professional. The tone should be clear and confident without being verbose, overcomplicated, childish, or generically AI-generated.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic AI-generated portfolio tropes: oversized vague hero claims, excessive cards, decorative gradients, childish emoji-heavy presentation, jargon-heavy self-description, and complicated layouts that obscure the writing. Avoid making the homepage feel like a resume dump or a marketing landing page.

## Design Principles

- Lead with a crisp identity: make the author, domain, and value of the site obvious in the first viewport.
- Show evidence quickly: surface writing and projects as proof, not as separate buried sections.
- Keep the system calm and readable: technical readers should be able to scan, decide, and continue without visual noise.
- Let personality come from selection and detail: use project/post choices, short notes, and restrained visual moments rather than decoration.
- Design for dual audiences: technical readers should find substance, while recruiters should find credibility and contact paths quickly.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use basic accessibility defaults: semantic structure, keyboard-accessible navigation, visible focus states, sufficient color contrast, dark-mode compatibility, and reduced-motion-safe interactions.
